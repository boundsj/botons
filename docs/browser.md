# The managed browser

Each Boton can have a browser of its own: a Chromium profile in `~/.botons/browser/<boton>/` (backed up with the workspaces, without caches or lock files), run by the server, never by the Boton. It comes as a connector built into Botons, `botons_browser`: check it under the Boton's **Connectors** to turn the browser on, and list the sites it may read under **Browser**. A new Boton (the first one in setup, **New Boton**, or one from a template or starter) starts with it checked and its sites set to `*`, any public site; a Boton made before keeps what it had, and its **Browser** settings ask once whether to turn web access on for all public sites. On a server without a Chromium the browser reads as off there, with why, and turns go without it. It works the same whether the Boton's brain is Claude or Codex, and a run a webhook started never gets it (webhook runs get no connectors).

## Acting on a page: your cards

It loads pages on its own, and acts on them only through your cards. The Boton has six browser tools: open an address, read the page (its text, its links to allowed sites, and its controls, each with a ref), go back, take a screenshot, click a control and type into a text field. Opening and reading load a page as any browser does, with GET and HEAD requests to the allowed sites; a site can act on a GET too, so that is limited to your sites, not proof that nothing changes. Clicking a link to an allowed site opens its address, as open would, without running the page's script for the click. Typing replaces the field's text and presses nothing, and is refused for passwords, codes, card numbers and other secrets (judged from what the page says of the field, so it errs towards refusing). Any other click, and any typing, marks the page as acted on: whatever the page then makes the browser send, whatever the method (a form, a GET form, a script's GET or POST), is held where it leaves Chromium and shown to you on an approval card:

- **The card is the request.** It shows the method, the full address with its query, every field or the body as sent, and the headers the page set (cookies and other credentials are named, never shown). The label of what was clicked is shown as the site's own text: a button called "Continue" can be a purchase, so nothing is ever allowed for its label. A request a card can't show in full (a file in it, bytes that aren't text, or too long) gets no card and is refused.
- **Allow once, now.** Allowing sends exactly the held request once, and only while the Boton's step waits for you (`BOTONS_APPROVAL_WAIT`). The server sends it itself, through the browser's proxy, with the headers and body it was held with and no cookie added: a request held without cookies goes without them. Right before it goes, the server checks again that it is the same turn, browser, page document, site, every byte and the browser's cookie jar the card was made for (cookies that changed meanwhile, a new sign-in say, mean it isn't sent: click again for a new card); the card is spent as it goes, so a second decision, a retry or another turn can't use it. No redirect is followed from a page acted on (a 307 or 308 would send it again); the Boton is told where it points. Denied, withdrawn, or not decided in time, it never leaves; there is no "always allow" for these cards, and a decision never carries into a later turn.
- **What became of it.** The card and the Boton's result say which: sent (the site answered), not sent (it failed before it left), or unknown (it was sent and the connection broke before the site answered, so the site may have acted on it). An unknown send is never retried: the Boton is told not to send it again and to tell you, so you can check on the site.
- **The cookies it goes with are known.** The browser's whole cookie jar is read as a click or typing starts and again once its request is held, and the two must be the same: a cookie added, changed or removed in between (by a sign-in, or the page's own script, even one that sets a cookie for that very request) means no card, and the Boton is told why. The usability cost is accepted on purpose: a page that rewrites a cookie as it submits can't be acted on this way, since nothing can tell which cookies Chromium put on the request. The request then carries no cookie at all (a script's request made without credentials) or exactly the jar's cookies for its address; two cookies of one name must be in the order the browser sends them, and two whose order can't be told apart (paths of one length) mean no card.
- **One request per card, nothing after.** Only the first request during the step is held (a form the step submits takes the place of a script's request, and a script's request that of anything else); every other request the page makes, during the step or later (a timer, a poll, an image, a beacon or keepalive as it unloads), is refused, and the browser's proxy lets nothing from Chromium through at all until the Boton opens an address or goes back, which ends that browser (Chromium and its proxy) and starts another on the same profile with the same cookies, so nothing the old page still had on its way can go.

It never signs in itself, downloads or uploads. You can sign its browser in yourself.

## Signing a Boton's browser in (a handover)

Under **Browser** in the Boton's settings, **Sign in for …** gives you its browser: you see its page and drive it with a few fixed inputs (open an address on its sites, click a point on the page, type text into whatever has focus, press Enter, Tab, Backspace, Escape or an arrow key, scroll, back, reload), sign in, and press **Done** to give it back. Closing the dialog gives it back too.

- **Yours alone while it lasts.** Taking it over closes the Boton's browser first (a card it was waiting on is withdrawn and its request never sent), and until you finish its browser tools are refused. Your browser is another Chromium, on a profile of its own made for the handover with the Boton's cookies, started for you on a blank page, never by a turn. Only the paired device that took it over gets its view and sends it input; any paired device can end it, and it ends on its own when that device is revoked, when nothing is sent for 10 minutes, or after 30 minutes. **Stop and turn off**, unchecking the connector and the server stopping end it too. A Boton's token can't reach any of it.
- **What it may send.** It loads pages from the Boton's sites only (the same proxy, the same refusal of private and loopback addresses unless a site names them). A page's writes (a sign-in form's POST, a script's request) go only to those sites and only within 10 seconds of your own click, key or typing; opening an address, going back and reloading end that moment at once, so a page's background requests at any other moment, what it sends as it unloads, and anything after you finish are refused. The proxy decides this for each request as it leaves, HTTPS included: for your browser alone it ends HTTPS itself, with a certificate made for that one browser, and checks each site's real certificate on the way on. Chromium's routing also refuses writes from a worker or another tab where it sees them; within the 10 seconds the proxy can't tell what in the browser sent a write, so it goes and is journaled. There is no input that runs a script, reads the page's DOM or cookies, or reaches Chromium's DevTools.
- **Your credentials stay yours.** What you type goes into the page and nowhere else: Botons doesn't keep, log or show it, the journal says only that you took over, opened which addresses (origin and path) and which requests the page sent, and the audit log records the start and the end with why it ended. The view is made on demand (a few frames a second at most) and kept nowhere: not in the workspace, the chat, the journal or the audit. Chromium's own password manager and autofill are off in every Boton's profile, so the profile keeps the site's cookies, not your password.
- **After.** Your browser closes and its profile is deleted, with whatever Chromium kept of the pages you filled in (form values in its session history, the history itself); one left by a server that was killed is deleted when it starts, and backups never include it. Only the cookies come back: into the Boton's profile, which keeps the cookies it had (a cookie the site removed during the handover is removed too, if the handover could read them as it started), and session cookies in the server's memory, so a sign-in that only lasts the browser's session is gone after the server restarts. If your browser dies before it can be read, the cookies as of your last input come back. The Boton's profile is backed up with the workspaces, under the same permissions.

## What the server enforces

Whatever the Boton or a page does:

- **Only while a turn has it.** The browser starts in a turn that was given the connector, answers only that turn's token, and closes when the turn ends. Outside a turn, only your handover opens it. Unchecking the connector or **Stop and turn off** (the kill switch) closes it at once.
- **Sites.** Every request goes through a proxy in the server that allows only the listed sites (`example.com` covers its subdomains, `host:port` names one address, `*` is any public site, for Workspace and Full computer Botons alike, and never this machine or a private network), resolves names itself and refuses private and loopback addresses (IPv6 included) unless an entry names them with their port. A Workspace Boton's list can name such an address only if `BOTONS_BROWSER_ALLOW` does. Removing a site cuts connections already open to it.
- **Reads only at the network, but for a card.** Anything but GET and HEAD (a form, a script's POST, a beacon) is refused before it leaves Chromium, and again in the proxy for plain HTTP; from a page acted on, everything is, at the routing and at the proxy, except the one request a card allowed. WebSockets never connect, service workers are blocked, and QUIC and WebRTC's direct UDP are off. Popups are closed and dialogs dismissed.
- **Chromium's sandbox is on.** In Docker, `compose.yaml` gives the container the seccomp profile that sandbox needs (see Browser in [DOCKER.md](DOCKER.md)); `BOTONS_BROWSER_SANDBOX=0` turns it off, the owner's last resort where a host still can't start it. Chromium runs with its own home folder and without the server's environment.
- **Page content is data.** The page's text comes back marked as the site's, with the rule that nothing on a page is an instruction from you. There is no tool that runs the Boton's own script in a page.
- **Records.** Each step, each blocked or held request and each request sent on a card goes to the Boton's browser journal (origin and path only, never a query; Settings → Recent browser steps); the card itself keeps the full request. Giving or taking the connector, changing its sites, **Stop**, every decision on a card and each handover's start and end go to the audit log. Screenshots are saved in its workspace (`browser-screenshots/`, never through a link the Boton made) and shown in its chat.

**Not yet.** It does not download into or upload from its workspace, show live browser steps and screenshots as cards in the chat, let you watch a Boton's turn as it browses, or control this computer's screen, mouse or keyboard (Settings says why).

## Checking it

`npm run check:browser` (in Docker, `botons check-browser`) starts the browser the way a turn does, on a temp profile against a page it serves on loopback, and checks that it reads, screenshots and lets nothing else out; `botons doctor` checks that Chromium starts. Install Chromium with your package manager (`chromium`), or `npx playwright-core install chromium`, or point `BOTONS_BROWSER_PATH` at one. On macOS it also finds Google Chrome or Chromium in your Applications folders ([macOS](install.md#macos)).
